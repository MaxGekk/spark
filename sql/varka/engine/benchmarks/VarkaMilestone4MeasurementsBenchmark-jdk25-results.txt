--- Reading these numbers (ratios by minimum-of-5, per PLAN_TASK_14.md 2.1's under-1.3x rule) ---

* Alignment: aligned beats misaligned at both widths, both runs - 1.68x by min at AVX-512
  (1.56x on the first run), 1.22x at 128-bit (1.25x on the first run). Real and reproducible,
  and scales with width: bigger where a load spans more of a cache line.
* Boolean materialization (a single comparison): toVector+and and blend are statistically tied at
  both widths across both runs - which one leads flips between runs (1.06x either way), always
  within this file's own noise band. Whether to skip materializing an int column at all is the
  real, width-dependent finding, reproduced on both runs: not materializing (bits-only straight
  to the packed bitmap) wins by 1.16-1.18x at AVX-512 but *loses* by 1.40-1.51x at 128-bit - the
  strategy should not be picked once and assumed to generalize across widths.
* Lane-width conversion: narrowest-drive and part-loop are tied at both widths on both runs
  (within 1.01x-1.05x either way) - narrowest-drive is the simpler build (one trip count) for the
  same throughput.
* Boolean trees ((a > b) AND (c < d)): mask-space and materialized-int are tied at AVX-512 on both
  runs (within 1.03x) but mask-space wins reproducibly at 128-bit - 1.24-1.37x across the two
  runs - never worse, sometimes decisively better.
* Division safety: blend-then-div beats masked-div at both widths on both runs - 1.08-1.12x at
  AVX-512, 1.18-1.19x at 128-bit by min. The one measurement whose margin stayed under 1.3x on
  every run; still consistent in direction and magnitude across all four data points (two widths
  times two runs), which is the interleaved-comparison the under-1.3x rule asks for.
