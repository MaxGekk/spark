VARKA-257's probe (scope item 62; VARKA-257.md 2 and 9): what a kernel meets when it is
warmed after N other kernels in the same JVM, which the cliff probe beside this file
(VarkaInliningCliff-jdk25-probe.txt) cannot see, since it forks one class per JVM.

Taken 16 October 2026 on the laptop (AMD Ryzen AI 9 HX PRO 370, 512-bit preferred species,
OpenJDK 25.0.4.1) with dev/varka_kernel_history.sh: fresh JVMs of VarkaKernelHistoryProbe,
each warming N kernels of the IR fuzz grammar (int at even positions, long at odd, seed
20260903 + fork) through production's warm-up worker, then the cliff probe's cheap tails
`year(d) + k` at 24 outputs, timed for 12 s, every kernel's answers compared with the
reference evaluator on the compiled instance. Two JVMs at a time on three cores each, pinned
to cores 12-17, beside other agents' work (the load is in each run's provenance). The
warm-up's deadline is 30 s: a history kernel RELEASED is one still allocating at 30 s, and
"with every loop C2" is one whose loop methods all had C2 code in use when it was released,
VARKA-295's cause 4. The kernel under test is slow above 40 ns a row (the median of its last
three seconds) or above 2048 bytes a call (its last second). "--prewarm off" is
-Dvarka.warmup.vectorTemplates=false; 128 bits is -XX:MaxVectorSize=16.
