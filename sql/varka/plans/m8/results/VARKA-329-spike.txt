VARKA-329 step 1: the hand-loop spike (TINYINT and SMALLINT compared as a selection, WIDENED against NATIVE).
Run 21 to 22 October 2026 (23:42 to 01:50 CEST) on the laptop: AMD Ryzen AI 9 HX PRO 370 (Zen 5, AVX-512 on 256-bit
units), OpenJDK 25.0.4.1+1-1-26.04.4-Ubuntu, Linux 7.0.0-38-generic, power profile `performance` and battery "Not
charging" before and after every run. Caches: L1d 48 KB and L2 1 MB a core, L3 24 MB in two complexes (16 MB for cpus 0-3 and 12-15, which the pin uses, and 8 MB
for the other eight cores, which it does not).
Code: master e18c8937384 plus the working tree of this commit (the benchmark class, the census change, which no loop
here uses, and for the 4 `-chk` runs the check of the compiled code described below); no emitter or IR code is touched. Class: sql/catalyst/src/test/java/org/apache/spark/sql/VarkaNarrowLaneBenchmark.java.

== command and pins ==
One JVM per run, launched directly with the Test javaOptions of `show catalyst/Test/javaOptions` (minus the second -Xmx4g)
and the Test fullClasspath, both read by sbt from the compiled tree:
  flock heavy.lock flock heavy2.lock taskset -c 0,12,13,14,15,1,2,3 java -Xmx4g <Test javaOptions> -XX:MaxVectorSize=<64|32|16> \
    -XX:CompileCommand=quiet "-XX:CompileCommand=dontinline,org.apache.spark.sql.VarkaNarrowLaneBenchmark::loop*" \
    -Dvarka.narrow.arms=<widened,int,scalar | native,int,scalar | widened,native,int,scalar> \
    -cp <Test classpath> org.apache.spark.sql.VarkaNarrowLaneBenchmark
(-XX:MaxVectorSize 64, 32, 16 give the 512-, 256- and 128-bit species; the widths are the species', not the datapath's.)
Before each run: 1-minute load under 3 and no other java (the script waits up to 5 minutes; every run started inside that),
both agent locks held for the whole JVM. Start and end times, loads and power state of each run are in "runs" below.
Each loop is a method of its own (dontinline), writes 64 rows to a long (one bit a row) with no tail, and is checked
against the scalar loop's words before it is timed. Row counts are multiples of 64, so this is the loop, not the epilogue,
and no validity bits or nulls are read.
Harness (VARKA-407's rule): each arm is driven at its own turn, just before it is timed, for at least 8 drives and then
until two consecutive drives are within 30 percent, with C2 idle after each drive and the loop method at level 4 in
Compiler.codelist. The 8 runs named `-fin` and `-chk` print one line per arm and rung: 568 lines, every one "level 4",
none "did not settle"; the 17 earlier runs ran the same rule before the print was added (and before the rung titles were
renamed), and none printed "did not settle". The check is weak in two ways: the code list does not mark an on-stack
replacement version, and a loop method is shared by every rung, so from the second rung on it is true on arrival; the steady
drive times are what show a rung settled. Every arm's words are checked against the scalar loop's before timing (in the
interpreter); the 4 `-chk` runs also check them after the arm is driven to compiled code, and none disagreed.
Timed: Spark's Benchmark, at least 3 iterations after a 1 s warm-up, 1 s minimum; an iteration is 2^27 rows (repeats of the
same column pair): Best Time is 1 ms for the fastest cell (NATIVE TINYINT at 512 bits, 16384 rows) and 3 to 130 ms for the
others, and the Rate column is the figure used. The last rung is not "past L3" for bytes here: a byte pair at 8388608 rows
is 16 MB, plus 1 MB of output words, against the 16 MB L3 of the pinned complex, so it is at that cache's edge; the short
pair (32 MB) and int pair (64 MB) are past it.
Arms: WIDENED = byte (short) load through the species a quarter (half) as wide as the int species (64 bits least), B2I (S2I)
to the int species in parts, int compare, toLong; at 128 bits the byte species is 64 bits (8 lanes, 2 parts). NATIVE =
preferred byte (short) species load, byte (short) compare, one toLong per group of lanes (1, 2, 4 groups a word at 512, 256,
128 bits for bytes). INT = the same predicate on INT columns. scalar = a Java loop packing the same word.

== one JVM or several, and the run-to-run modes ==
The all-arms runs (one JVM with WIDENED, NATIVE and INT loops: the two-species case of the plan) agree with the separate runs
to within 10 percent in every cell at 512 and 256 bits except one (`b < i` at 16384 rows and 512 bits, 11 percent). Nothing
boxed and nothing slowed 6x to 13x: no second-species penalty was seen in any run.
At 128 bits the WIDENED loops are bimodal from JVM to JVM, in two cells that move opposite ways. SMALLINT `s1 < s2` WIDENED
reads 5.8 G rows/s in 6 of 9 JVMs (w128-wid-r1, r2, r3 and w128-all-r2, fin, chk) and 7.2 G, equal to the INT arm, in 3
(w128-wid-fin, w128-wid-chk, w128-all-r1). TINYINT `greatest(b1,b2) < i` WIDENED reads 6.1 G in
5 JVMs (the all-arms runs and w128-wid-r1) and 5.4 G in 4 (w128-wid-r2, r3, fin, chk). All four rungs of a cell move together,
so it is a property of the JVM's compile, not of a rung; the cause was not looked for (no PrintAssembly). NATIVE and INT do not
show it. The medians in the tables sit on the more common mode, the best-rate ratios on the faster one, and both are given.
WIDENED with 128-bit byte loads (4 parts at 128 bits, 2 at 256) against the 64-bit form, medians over the separate runs: 1.10 to
1.13 at 256 bits and 0.97 to 0.99 at 128 bits (1.02 to 1.05 in the all-arms JVMs): a gain at 256 bits, none the runs separate
from noise at 128.
